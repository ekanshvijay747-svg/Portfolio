Air Cadets

103 Thunderbird Squadron

I've been an active member of 103 Thunderbird Squadron for four years and have advanced to the rank of Sergeant. As a ground school instructor, I teach scholarship exam topics like air rules and procedures, propeller and engine instruments, and identifying Canadian satellites. As Band 2IC, I work with the Drum Major on rehearsals, attendance, and uniform inspection, and coach junior cadets one on one on tie knots, boot care, and badge placement.

Rank: Sergeant
Qualifications: Advanced Aviation, Aeronautical Radio licence, and First Aid certification.

Air Cadets

Wingmate: Air Cadet Hub

Teaching ground school, I saw scholarship exam prep boil down to “memorize to get 60%.” Cadets could define an altimeter but couldn't connect it to how cold fronts and barometric pressure skew its reading. Wingmate makes cadet knowledge easy to find and links related topics together. I started with the lowest-risk area, uniform standards, so version 1 covers ironing, boot polishing, badge placement, and uniform codes, with video guides and a discussion forum. I built it over the summer and presented it to the squadron's officers, who are reviewing it now. Phase 2 is a knowledge graph for aviation theory.

Robotics

My VEX Story

Five years, every role

I've competed in VEX since elementary school, starting with plastic VEX IQ builds and moving up to metal VEX V5. I've worked every role a team needs. This year I'm the designer, co-builder, and author of our Engineering Notebook.

Innovate Award

In one season I reworked our intake to sort blocks by colour. A small gap in the rubber-band wall let only the smaller blue blocks through, so the robot could drop one colour at a time and earn the uniformity bonus. That design won us the Innovate Award.

Judges Award

Two days before one competition, our intake kept missing balls, the drivetrain lacked torque, and my teammate wanted to skip. I stayed after class to talk it through. We split up the judging interview and were honest about where the robot stood. We didn't do well on the field, but we won the Judges Award for resilience.

Robotics

VEX V5 CAD Prototyping

VEX V5 builds are metal, so mistakes are costly to fix once parts are cut. I taught myself CAD to prototype before we build. Over the summer I modeled our drivetrain and intake, which set the gear ratios, motor layout, and robot dimensions ahead of build time. Our drivetrain was running within a week of the robotics lab opening, and the head start let the team prototype two mechanisms, a cascade lift and a double reverse four-bar.

Robotics

VEX Engineering Notebook

I author our Engineering Notebook, documenting each iteration, its strengths and weaknesses, and our testing. Our team used to scramble in the final week before competitions, so I suggested a rule: commit to goals on Monday and check progress on Friday. The first weeks were a disaster, with goals forgotten, vague, or too ambitious. Instead of dropping the idea, we switched to smaller written goals planned around school work. We were still chaotic, but we started finishing what we committed to, and that steady progress eased the final-week pressure and improved the robot we brought to competition.

Academics

DeltaLog

I built DeltaLog to track and categorize my mistakes for the AMC 10 and competitive mathematics. Instead of simply studying harder, I used this system to log specific divergence points and cognitive blindspots. By tracking these trends over time, I discovered that my biggest contributors to lost points were perceptual misreads and calculation slips caused by rushing. Logging these specific errors allowed me to strategically adjust my test-taking process.

Process Optimization

This is the AMC 10 Pre-Flight Checklist I designed to optimize my exam execution. I realized that my mock test scores stalled not because of a lack of math skills, but due to an unstructured approach to solving problems. I implemented a strict routine, including reading questions twice, constraint logging, and variable normalization. These process-driven changes, combined with 5:00 AM study blocks, significantly improved my performance by eliminating unforced errors.
